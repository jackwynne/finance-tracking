Finance app review and improvement plan

Direction updated on 2 October 2026 following the user's request. Build this personal app around two questions: how much am I spending and broadly on what, and what is my exposure by country, industry and asset class? ChatGPT outside the app should do much of the updating. Include all assets in the model. Keep Convex, TanStack Start and the existing import staging. The earlier technical review below remains implementation context.

Build one update service for validation, preview, apply and undo. Prove its contract through file updates, then expose it through a personal MCP connection so routine ChatGPT updates do not require file round trips. Codex can use the same contract through a CLI. Keep file download and upload as a fallback, without requiring a terminal or individual transaction edits.

Start with transaction classification

Fix the spending rules and build ChatGPT export, review and bulk apply. Then record current asset balances and add country, industry and asset-class breakdowns. Use a shared update contract for both areas.

Personal app direction

Use three main destinations: Spending, Exposure and Updates. Transactions, merchants, accounts and source files become supporting detail. Keep setup small and specific to ANZ, CommBank, Smart, Simplicity and Hostplus. A personal category catalogue and reusable rules are more useful here than a large generic budgeting system.

Spending

Start with this month's spending, the previous comparable period, a monthly trend and broad category bars. Suggested groups are housing and bills, groceries, eating out, transport, shopping, health, travel and other. ChatGPT can propose changes to these groups. Clicking a group opens its transactions. Keep unclassified spending visible as an amount and count rather than hiding it from the total.

Import both bank accounts in their original currencies. Offer a combined NZD view initially, with an AUD reporting option and original amounts in transaction detail. Store dated FX rates and identify missing conversions. Compare month-to-date with the same elapsed portion of the previous month. A partial account import must show its coverage dates so a lower total is not mistaken for lower spending.

Movement between ANZ and CommBank is a transfer even when the amounts differ after conversion. Store both legs and the conversion evidence; any identifiable fee is spending. Investment contributions and debt principal payments are separate movements. Refunds offset spending, reimbursements need an explicit treatment, and loan interest remains spending. Do not classify every outgoing payment as consumption.

Exposure

Start with total recorded assets, liabilities, net worth and accessible versus retirement balances. Follow with country, industry and asset-class breakdowns, then holdings and their sources. Default allocation percentages use recorded gross asset value, with debts shown alongside it. A net-worth denominator can be misleading for allocation, especially with borrowing. Filters for all assets, investments and retirement should state their denominator.

Treat KiwiSaver and super as account restrictions, not asset classes. Break them into their actual underlying shares, bonds, property and cash when supported by dated disclosures. Cash remains cash; an industry view keeps non-industry assets in an explicit not-applicable bucket. Country initially means the provider's country classification for investments and bank jurisdiction for deposits. Label that basis and keep currency exposure separate. Do not claim revenue geography from company domicile.

Support manual dated balances for property, other assets and debts if present, without assuming the user owns them. Record ownership share and valuation source. The full model includes these categories even though the supplied inventory currently confirms only bank cash, ETFs and retirement funds. Unknown balances and unresolved fund weights remain visible. Coverage is measured separately for country, industry and asset class, against the full recorded asset value, not just the portion successfully classified.

Read-only bank feeds

Prefer automatic read-only bank feeds where a suitable personal connection is available, with exports as a fallback. ANZ supports consented sharing of balances and transaction history, with approval and revocation through goMoney. Its direct access is limited to ANZ-approved or MBIE-accredited third parties. These facts were checked against ANZ Data Sharing on 2 October 2026. An intermediary is a more practical starting point than seeking accreditation for this app.

The user selected Akahu for the initial ANZ connection on 2 October 2026. Use its personal API. The personal tier is free and limited to the user's own account. It supports account information and daily refresh, and excludes payments and webhooks. Akahu's official open banking documentation describes bank-side authorisation without credential sharing, but also says it retains classic integrations. Confirm that official ANZ connectivity is enabled for the personal app before linking the account. If it is unavailable, retain bank exports and revisit the provider choice. Read-only access in Koru alone is insufficient if the upstream connection requires broader account access.

CommBank supports Australian CDR data sharing through accredited recipients and providers collecting data through them. Assess an Australian intermediary separately; the NZ connection must not be assumed to cover CommBank. Keep its export workflow until an eligible provider and personal-use terms are verified. No provider account has been created or linked.

Bank-feed credentials stay on the app server and grant account-data access only. ChatGPT receives selected records from Koru, not bank credentials or bank access tokens. Do not request payment consent or implement payment endpoints. Imported feeds and exports use the same source-aware deduplication and reporting rules. Show the last successful sync, consent status and covered transaction dates; a failed sync preserves stored data.

Akahu implementation steps

  1. Confirm personal-tier eligibility and official ANZ connectivity. Set up the user's personal Akahu app with account-information access only. Complete bank consent in goMoney. No payment permission is required.
  2. Store the Akahu app and user tokens in server-side secrets. Bind the connection to the authenticated profile. Keep tokens out of browser responses, ChatGPT exports and logs.
  3. Import ANZ accounts, dated balances and all available transaction pages through a server-side job. Match the feed to the existing ANZ account before creating another account. Retain provider IDs and source evidence. Reconcile overlap with earlier file imports so the same transaction is counted once.
  4. Schedule a daily sync and add a manual refresh that respects Akahu's documented one-hour rest period. Use polling because personal apps have no webhooks. Keep sync progress on the server and make retries safe after interruption. Preserve accepted classifications when bank data is refreshed.
  5. Show connection status, last successful sync, history coverage and errors in Updates. Provide a disconnect control and handle external revocation. Keep statement imports available for missing history and CommBank.

Complete the first connection when the imported ANZ balance and sample transactions reconcile to bank evidence, all available pages are read, and repeat syncs or overlapping exports create no duplicates. Verify that revocation stops new imports, failures preserve existing records, and no credential appears in client responses or update packs. This is a planned integration; no Akahu account or bank connection has been configured in this task.

Files, emails and statements

The user does not want ChatGPT to access any part of a bank or investment account that can make transactions. Use copies of bank exports, statements and the monthly email reporting new share purchases. No bank or broker login is needed. Any future direct connection is to this app's record-keeping functions only, with no trading, payment or withdrawal capability. Public fund disclosures are a separate read-only data source.

  1. Download a task-specific update pack with personal category definitions, confirmed merchant rules, relevant records, stable IDs and instructions. Include only the context needed for the task.
  2. Give ChatGPT the pack and new bank exports, purchase emails, statements or fund disclosures. Ask it to classify spending, extract dated holdings and balances, or prepare a sourced fund breakdown. It returns a changes file and questions for items it cannot resolve.
  3. Upload the changes file. The app shows before/after amounts, affected records, stale revisions, duplicate proposals and unresolved items. Apply the reviewed changes as one recorded job with undo.
  4. Reuse accepted rules next time. Future update packs focus on new activity and exceptions, with a full-ledger export available for broader review. Public fund sources can later refresh through provider adapters.

The monthly routine

  • Sync bank activity through verified read-only feeds where available; otherwise download ANZ and CommBank exports. Use confirmed merchant rules first and ask ChatGPT about exceptions. Show each account's covered dates and any missing FX rates.
  • Supply a copy of the share-purchase email as saved email, PDF, pasted text or a screenshot. Preserve its source and let ChatGPT extract the purchase date, fund identity, units, currency, amount and any stated fees or reference. Only populate fields actually present. Ask for missing information if it affects holdings.
  • Review one update summary, then apply it. Match purchase references and retained source hashes where available. Cross-source duplicates, such as the same purchase in an email and a later CSV, require matching by account, instrument, date, units and amount. Link supporting evidence instead of adding a second purchase.
  • Use occasional dated statements to reconcile holdings and update KiwiSaver, Hostplus and bank balances. Add new purchases to the latest verified position snapshot. A purchase amount is not a current valuation; dated prices and fund disclosures refresh value and exposure separately.

The email's exact fields and format have not been inspected. Start with user-supplied copies rather than mailbox access. Email-only activity may miss sales, fees, reinvestments, switches or corporate actions, so show the last reconciliation date and unresolved differences. A manually supplied email should be enough to prepare a draft; it should not imply that a complete holdings history has been recovered.

ChatGPT should prepare structured proposals; deterministic app code calculates money, FX and allocations. The update contract should support transaction classification, explicit reporting treatment, statement balances, position snapshots and dated fund allocations. Each change references its source, record revision and scope. Missing dates, ambiguous currencies and incomplete extracts become questions rather than invented values. A screenshot can start a draft, but a dated statement is the reconciliation source.

A personal custom GPT with GPT Actions is an alternative REST-based connection if MCP is unavailable on the user's account. Prefer the MCP route below and avoid maintaining both integrations initially. No direct connection is implemented.

A small set of app tools

Define stable tool names, validated inputs and structured outputs. Each tool serves a user task rather than exposing raw database operations. These names are proposed contracts, not existing endpoints.

Tool Purpose and contract
get_review_context Return the authenticated profile, reporting preferences, category definitions, confirmed rules, account and instrument IDs, last review and data freshness. Include a context version so proposals identify the rules they used.
list_transactions Filter by date, account, merchant and review state. Return a bounded page with stable IDs, revisions, original amounts, classification evidence and a continuation cursor. State whether more records remain.
get_spending_summary Calculate the selected period and comparison in the app, including broad categories, unclassified amounts, FX dates and account coverage. Never infer a full-period total from one transaction page.
get_exposure_summary Calculate country, industry or asset-class allocation for a specified asset scope and reporting currency. Return the denominator, source dates, unresolved values and contributing holdings. Link detail to owned source records.
get_record_evidence Fetch selected owned transactions, holdings or source excerpts by returned IDs. Keep results bounded and omit credentials and unnecessary account identifiers.
stage_updates Accept typed classification, rule, reporting-treatment, purchase, position or sourced allocation proposals with revisions and an idempotency key. Save a draft job and unresolved questions. Do not change accepted records.
preview_updates Validate a draft job and return exact before/after changes, reporting effects, conflicts, counts and a preview ID bound to the draft revision and content hash. Provide a Koru review link.
apply_updates Apply an authorized preview by its ID and hash, with an idempotency key. Recheck record revisions and return a durable job ID and receipt. Retrying the same request returns the same job.
get_update_job Return progress, accepted and rejected counts, unresolved questions, source references and the undo reference. ChatGPT can resume an interrupted conversation using the job ID.
undo_updates Restore a selected job's previous values where subsequent edits do not conflict. Return restored and conflicted records, with a receipt. Undo changes Koru records only.

The transaction review workflow

The shared workflow is context, proposals, validation, preview, apply and undo. The personal MCP connection is the intended routine interface. The file workflow below proves the same contract and remains a fallback for ChatGPT or Codex. Neither route requires individual transaction edits in the UI.

  1. Import bank files and preserve their raw records. Reuse confirmed merchant aliases and rules, while retaining the source of each classification.
  2. Run an authenticated export against an explicitly selected deployment. Export the complete ledger as a versioned review bundle, with a manifest, category catalogue, merchant groups and numbered transaction chunks. Include representative evidence in the merchant groups as well as every transaction in the chunks.
  3. Codex reads the bundle and writes a proposals file containing only decisions. Each proposal can set a merchant default, classify explicit transaction IDs, propose an alias merge, propose a split or reporting treatment, or leave an item unresolved with one specific question.
  4. Validate the proposals locally, then run a server-side dry run against the same deployment. Write a preview report to a file containing affected counts, before/after values, explanations, conflicts and unresolved questions. Review that file in Codex. No financial records change during this step.
  5. Apply the reviewed proposal file through an authenticated, idempotent batch job. Save an apply receipt with job ID, accepted/rejected counts and an undo reference. Undo is also callable from the file workflow and checks for edits made after apply.

Use a small Node CLI around the same owner-scoped Convex functions that an optional upload/download screen would call. Commands should select the deployment explicitly and derive the owner from authentication, rather than trusting an owner ID in the file. Keep authentication outside the exported bundle. A production bundle must be rejected by another deployment even if IDs happen to match. Read-only commands may run immediately; an apply command must reference the exact file hash and dry-run result reviewed by the user. This review does not introduce a production write or reset.

The proposed file layout is manifest.json, categories.json, merchants.ndjson and transactions-0001.ndjson onward. Codex returns proposals.ndjson and questions.md. The CLI writes preview.json and apply-receipt.json. These are planned contracts, not commands already implemented. The manifest records contract version, deployment identity, owner/export identity, counts and chunk hashes. Every transaction keeps its revision so applying an old file cannot overwrite a later manual correction.

The input must include stable IDs, a bundle ID, record revisions, category definitions and examples, signed amounts serialized as decimal strings, currencies, dates, account labels, raw descriptions and existing classification provenance. Merchant examples help triage, but they must not replace the full transaction export. Mask account identifiers and omit authentication credentials.

Include category definitions and examples in the file contract. Accept catalogue changes as explicit proposals; an optional app editor can use the same contract. Give unresolved classification one explicit state instead of relying on both a missing category and a seeded Uncategorized category. Show loading, query errors and a completed empty result separately; an empty filtered page should not say that no transactions have ever been imported.

The response must refer to IDs from that bundle and include a rationale and confidence level. Confidence is a review aid, not proof that a category is correct. Omitted items remain unchanged. An unresolved proposal is distinct from an intentional request to clear a category. Do not require Codex to repeat or reorder the entire input document.

Each proposal must state its scope explicitly: one transaction, listed transactions, unclassified matches, or future matching transactions. A transaction override must never change a merchant default. A merchant default must never overwrite a manual override. Broader reclassification should preview exactly what it will replace.

Separate category from reporting treatment. Transfers, investment contributions, refunds, income and expenses need different reporting behaviour. Permit splits that sum exactly to the transaction amount. A supermarket default is useful; an Amazon default needs transaction exceptions. Transfers and refunds should be reviewable suggestions until confirmed by the user or a sufficiently specific confirmed rule.

Store proposals separately from accepted decisions. Keep batch progress on the server so applying a large file survives navigation and interrupted requests. Check revisions again when applying; do not overwrite edits made after export. Reject unknown IDs, cross-owner references, duplicate decisions and invalid split totals. Make retrying a batch idempotent. Undo restores the recorded previous values and reports conflicts with subsequent edits.

Add a project classification skill after this file contract is implemented. It should tell Codex how to read the bundle, use the category catalogue, produce validated proposals and list unresolved questions. A small local validator should check the response before server-side dry run and apply. Test the file round trip on an isolated deployment first, then export from production. Production classification uses the current ledger, with no reset or reimport required.

A portfolio model that supports real holdings

Keep personal ownership separate from public fund composition. Owning units of Smart TWF does not mean the user directly owns units of Vanguard VT. VT is a child in TWF's breakdown.

Data Purpose
Instruments and identifiers Stable identity for Smart ETFs, Vanguard/iShares child funds, Simplicity funds, companies and other assets. Store exchange plus ticker, ISIN or provider ID where available. Screenshot FundNZ codes are aliases until mapped to official identifiers.
Investment accounts Separate Smart, KiwiSaver and Australian super, with currency and retirement restrictions.
Investment activity Typed buys, sells, contributions, withdrawals, dividends, reinvestments, tax, fees, fund switches and corporate actions, linked to account and instrument. Preserve provider descriptions.
Position snapshots Statement holdings or explicit opening balances on a given date. Needed when imported history is incomplete.
Prices and exchange rates Dated market prices, fund unit prices, manual valuations and currency conversion, each with source and timestamp.
Fund snapshots and constituent rows A dated fund composition, with child instruments, weights, metadata and the original source document. Keep constituent rows in a separate table.
Import and refresh jobs Durable status, progress, validation issues and retry state for private imports and public provider refreshes.

Calculate positions from a statement snapshot plus subsequent activity, or a complete activity history. Reconcile against provider running balances. Do not sum the latest 500 transactions or assume an incomplete history starts at zero. A Simplicity export without a fund column must ask which fund and account it belongs to. A file spanning a fund switch requires a switch date or separate mappings.

Use exact decimal arithmetic for units, prices and weights. Retain integer minor units for money and round at documented valuation boundaries. Model activity and job states as discriminated unions and derive TypeScript types from the Convex validators. Validate external files once at the boundary.

The first portfolio screen should show each holding's units or statement balance, value, valuation date, source and account. Use NZD as the initial reporting currency, while displaying Hostplus's original AUD balance and the conversion date. Missing prices or balances remain unknown. A partial total must say which holdings are excluded. Show cash, investments, retirement assets and liabilities separately before combining net worth.

Link bank investment payments to investment contributions when possible, so they are not counted twice. Employee KiwiSaver deductions must not be deducted from imported net salary again. Employer contributions belong in the investment activity view without becoming ordinary bank income.

Use bounded Convex actions for network work and internal mutations for writes. Scheduled refreshes should retry idempotently and avoid simultaneous publication for the same fund.

Poll according to each source's actual publication cadence. Prices may update on business days; composition may update monthly, quarterly or less often. Display those dates separately. Historical views must select composition and prices known at the requested date. Never apply today's holdings to a historical chart without an explicit estimate label.

Calculating exposure and overlap

For each holding, multiply its portfolio value by every fund weight along the path to the underlying asset. Sum all paths to a company across Smart funds, KiwiSaver and other holdings.

For example, a hypothetical NZ$10,000 TWF position with a 99% VT allocation and a 4% Apple weight in VT gives NZ$396 of Apple exposure. Both percentages are illustrative. Actual calculations use the stored dated disclosures.

Use security identifiers to match holdings and a separate issuer identity to combine share classes when showing company totals. Keep security detail available. Add country, sector and asset class only when the source supports them. A US-listed fund is not automatically US company exposure, and an Irish-domiciled ETF is not Irish economic exposure. Keep currency denomination and currency hedging separate.

Stop at an unresolved child fund and report its value as unresolved. Detect cycles. Preserve cash, derivatives, liabilities and disclosed signed weights; holdings can exceed 100% of net assets with offsetting positions. Do not normalize incomplete holdings to 100% or silently remove those offsets. Sector classifications from different providers need an explicit mapping, with unknown classifications retained.

The portfolio should answer:

  • How much is held in each fund and account?
  • Which companies account for the largest combined exposure?
  • How much is in each country, sector and asset class?
  • Which funds contribute to a company's exposure?
  • How much of the portfolio has a resolved breakdown, and how old are its sources?
  • What is accessible now, and what is in KiwiSaver or super?

Start with a holdings table, allocation bars and a searchable company table. Clicking a company should show every contributing fund and its calculation. Add a map only if it helps answer a question the table cannot.
